# FLOODSHADOW 2.0 — Jury demonstration

FLOODSHADOW connects weather forecasts, territorial context, documented impacts and verification around the same rainfall episode in Guayaquil. The application, navigation, evidence passports, forms and explanations are in English. Original place names and source records retain their source language. All sector polygons are visible by default; incident points are an additional overlay.

1. **Overview:** select October 24–25. The source lists 68 urban water-accumulation locations and other incidents. The map shows colored, selectable sector polygons and eleven candidate intersections; only seven have alternatives consistent with one provisional sector; broad or ambiguous locations remain unresolved. These points do not delineate flooded neighborhoods.
2. **Before:** inspect an archived ECMWF forecast and the two-factor vigilance index. Explain both contributions. The index is exploratory, not a calibrated flood probability. The historical index was reconstructed later and cannot be claimed as a forecast issued by this platform before the event.
3. **During:** select October 19. Compare the five published station accumulations, which differ by location. Move the hourly slider to explore model output. Published station values are not fabricated hourly curves, and model estimates are not gauge measurements.
4. **After:** keep the same selected sector while alternating the map. Inspect the source, location quality and missing evidence. Zero valid inspections means the proportion affected among inspected sectors is unavailable, not zero damage.
5. **Prospective capture:** select the saved 72-hour forecast. Its inputs and assessment were stored before the target window, with a timestamp and content checksum. The provider's exact issue time is unavailable. This capture must await independent observations before evaluation.
6. **Shadow Detection:** examine a high-index sector without sufficient evidence. It is a candidate for gathering information, not a confirmed flood. Actual evidence can be recorded and reviewed; point observations and sector-wide checks have different scopes.
7. **Adaptive verification:** open the clearly separated Laboratory. Inspect a synthetic sector and show how positive or negative evidence updates neighboring estimates. These experimental incidents are never presented as current Guayaquil observations.

Be candid about performance: none of the six evaluable candidate sectors in the October 24–25 collection exceeds the exploratory vigilance threshold; one additional candidate lacks an index. This is not a valid forecast-recall estimate, but it exposes limitations. Municipal infrastructure queries failed, field-validation data are insufficient, and the real-event index is not operationally validated.

The contribution is an integrated, auditable workflow that preserves uncertainty, original forecasts and evidence boundaries while keeping experimental research available for evaluation.
