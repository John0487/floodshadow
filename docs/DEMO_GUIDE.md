# Explore a Rainfall Event

Start `npm run demo:local` and open http://127.0.0.1:5174/. Keep Prepared Demo Mode selected. It uses saved real sources and a local boundary map. The computer may be disconnected from the internet; localhost must remain reachable. This is different from stopping the local server.

1. **Select an event.** Start with October 24–25. The published list describes locations; it does not establish that 68 neighborhoods flooded.
2. **Before the Rain.** Show expected rainfall and places worth checking. For past storms the index is a later reconstruction, not a prediction issued beforehand. Millimeters measure rain accumulation, not street flood depth.
3. **During the Rain.** Play the hourly weather-model estimates. Select October 19 to show the five separately published station totals. Do not call modeled hours station observations.
4. **After the Rain.** Review blue rainfall context, source points and Area Alerts & Findings. Explain Documented Impact versus a reviewed Confirmed Impact.
5. **Explore an area.** Search Urdesa to isolate 89 smaller areas. Use the general-area selector to navigate municipal quadrants. A technical subarea name is a navigation aid, not an invented neighborhood. Samborondón remains separate context without inherited storm estimates.
6. **Scenario Explorer.** Move rain amount to 200%, then 0%. Keep the simulation banner visible. Watch priorities change against the original fixed rainfall reference. Missing cells remain unknown; no real incident is created.
7. **Key findings.** Return to real evidence and generate the Rainfall Impact Report. Show its sources, missing observations and limits. Real checks require actual evidence; never enter a made-up inspection during a presentation.

The optional research laboratory demonstrates positive and negative synthetic inspections updating other estimates. It does not prove local flood-prediction accuracy. The current beta collects evidence and updates assessments; supervised retraining is a future capability.

## Short pitch

“FLOODSHADOW helps Guayaquil understand a rainfall event from expectation to evidence. We connect expected rain, territorial context and reported locations, then show where information is still missing. The map uses recognizable names and keeps source precision visible. Our scenario explorer lets a presenter ask what might change under different rain assumptions without creating fake incidents. Reviewed evidence is saved locally with its history. We do not claim to predict every flood: our contribution is a clear, traceable way to decide what needs a closer look.”

The English hackathon PDF and video requirements remain applicable. The presentation document supports a pitch; it does not replace recording and timing the required video or submitting through the committee’s chosen channel.
# Connected interface

The new main screen uses three concise sidebar stages. **Before** shows provider-supplied hourly rain chance, the forecast map and an optional El Niño context panel. **During** shows estimated rain intensity and the airport observation. **After** highlights recent source-mentioned areas, with a separate forecast-review drawer. Open **Sources** for provenance and limitations. On mobile, the three stages move to a top bar; the Sources drawer also links to Forecast review and Saved storms. Use **Saved storms** for the prepared historical demonstration described below.
